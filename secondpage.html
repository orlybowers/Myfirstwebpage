Adoption

The middle era of Bitcoin was defined by extreme market cycles, internal governance debates, and a shift toward wider public awareness. Following the catastrophic collapse of Mt. Gox in 2014 due to a massive hack, Bitcoin entered a prolonged bear market that tested its resilience. Despite the setbacks, development continued, leading to the fierce "Blocksize Wars" of 2017, a civil war within the community over how to scale the network. This culminated in a hard fork that created Bitcoin Cash, leaving the original Bitcoin network dedicated to preserving its security and scarcity.

Late 2017 brought Bitcoin into the mainstream spotlight as retail investor FOMO (fear of missing out) propelled the price to nearly $20,000. Although a brutal 80% market correction followed in 2018, the underlying infrastructure matured significantly during this downtime, seeing the introduction of the Lightning Network for faster payments and the launch of the first regulated futures markets. When the COVID-19 pandemic struck in 2020, massive global central bank money printing shifted the narrative surrounding Bitcoin from an experimental digital cash system to a macro hedge against inflation, often referred to as "digital gold."
