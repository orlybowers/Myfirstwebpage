Genesis

The foundations of Bitcoin were laid during the global financial crisis when Satoshi Nakamoto published the whitepaper titled "Bitcoin: A Peer-to-Peer Electronic Cash System" in October 2008. On 3 January 2009, the network officially went live with the mining of the genesis block, which contained a hardcoded message referencing a British newspaper headline about bank bailouts. In its infancy, Bitcoin had no monetary value and was primarily transacted among cryptography enthusiasts. The first commercial transaction occurred in May 2010, when a programmer famously paid 10,000 Bitcoins for two pizzas, establishing its earliest real-world valuation.

As the concept gained traction, early infrastructure began to form. The establishment of Mt. Gox in 2010 provided a centralized exchange for users to trade Bitcoin for fiat currency, significantly increasing liquidity. By 2013, Bitcoin experienced its first massive bull run, driven by growing media coverage and the rise of alternative use cases, crossing the $1,000 milestone for the first time. However, this era was also marked by controversy, as Bitcoin became the primary currency for the "Silk Road," an anonymous darknet marketplace, drawing intense scrutiny from global regulators.
